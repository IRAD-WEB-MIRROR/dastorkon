Chak-chak was always on the table when my aunts came to Orto-Ayyl for a name day, and we children were sent off to the far room so we wouldn't pick at the mound before the guests had sat. The steppe cooks of the north and our grandmothers in the valley both make it, and nobody in the family agrees whose is better.

Now I make it in my Talas kitchen with honey from the bazaar and [butter from Talas Süt](https://talas-sut.com/products.html#butter), and Aruu counts the pieces as I fry them. Chop some [walnuts](../../pantry/#walnuts) for the top, my dear, and trust me on this: the syrup is the part to watch.

## Method

1. Make the dough: whisk the eggs with the melted butter, milk and salt, stir in the flour, then knead for 5 minutes until smooth and firm.
2. Rest the dough: cover it and leave it for 30 minutes at room temperature.
3. Cut the sticks: roll the dough 5 mm thick, cut it into strips 1 cm wide, then cut the strips into pieces about the length of a finger.
4. Fry the sticks: heat the oil to 170 °C and fry in small batches for 3 to 4 minutes until deep golden, then lift out onto paper towels. This takes about 25 minutes.
5. Make the syrup: bring the honey, sugar and water to a gentle boil in a wide pan and simmer for 8 minutes, stirring, until it thickens slightly and drops slowly from the spoon.
6. Toss the sticks: add the fried sticks to the warm syrup off the heat and stir quickly until every piece is coated.
7. Shape the mound: tip it onto a platter, shape it into a mound with wet hands and scatter the walnuts over it.
8. Let it set: leave it for 60 minutes until firm enough to break apart by hand.
