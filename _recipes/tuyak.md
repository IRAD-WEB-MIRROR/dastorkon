In Orto-Ayyl, after a wedding, the women who had cooked all night kept one more pot going for the morning, and my chong apa's was always tuyak. By dawn it filled the house with a warm, rich smell, and the tired men came in from the yard to sit in front of it with their sleeves rolled and their hands around a bowl. Nobody spoke until the garlic was stirred in.

In Chicago I never dared simmer a pot like this in my small apartment. Here in Talas my aunt sent me to the bazaar butcher, who cut the trotters for me without a question. I buy the kurut from [Talas Süt](https://talas-sut.com/products.html#kurut), dissolve it in the broth and crush the garlic in a mortar, my dear. Aruu held her nose over the pot. Then she asked for a second bowl.

## Method

1. Soak the trotters. Put them in a big pot of cold water and leave them for 8 hours or overnight in the fridge, changing the water twice, until it runs almost clear.
2. Blanch the trotters. Drain them, cover with fresh cold water, bring to the boil and boil hard for 15 minutes, until grey scum rises.
3. Scrub the trotters. Drain them, rinse under the tap and scrub off any scum and loose hair with a knife and your hands; wash the pot too.
4. Start the broth. Return the trotters to the clean pot with the 4 L of cold water, the onions, carrot, bay leaves and peppercorns, and bring slowly to a gentle boil.
5. Skim and simmer. Skim off the foam for the first 15 minutes, then lower the heat until the surface only just trembles, cover with the lid slightly open and cook for 6 hours, until the meat falls off the bone.
6. Season the broth. Stir in the salt for the last half hour, then taste and add more if the broth needs it.
7. Make the garlic. While the pot finishes, crush the garlic cloves in a mortar with a pinch of salt, then thin them with a ladle of hot broth.
8. Dissolve the kurut. Break the kurut balls into 200 ml of hot broth in a bowl, then stir with a spoon for 10 minutes until they melt into a cloudy white liquid.
9. Rest the pot. Turn off the heat, take out the onions, carrot and bay leaves and let the broth stand for 10 minutes before you ladle it.
10. Serve hot. Fill each bowl with trotter meat and broth, add garlic and kurut to taste, tear flatbread into the bowl and finish with dill, coriander and black pepper.
