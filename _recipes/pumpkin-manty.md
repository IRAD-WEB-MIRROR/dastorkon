My chong apa kept every Wednesday and Friday, and on those days her kitchen smelled of pumpkin and hot oil instead of lamb. She tucked diced pumpkin into the dough so sweet that the children forgot there was no meat. In Chicago I made these on fast days for my church friends, and we ate them standing at my tiny counter, laughing at the steam on my glasses.

Today is Wednesday, so I made them here in Talas for Aruu and for my aunt, who came by bus to check my pleats. They are cousins of my [manty](../manty/) with meat, only softer. Dribble the chile oil over them and sprinkle on a few [walnuts](../../pantry/#walnuts), my dear, and nobody will ask where the meat went.

## Method

1. Make the dough. Mix the flour and salt in a bowl, add the warm water and the oil, then knead for 10 minutes into a firm, smooth dough.
2. Rest the dough. Cover it with a damp cloth and leave it for 30 minutes, so it rolls out without springing back. Make the filling during the rest; only the last 15 minutes are idle.
3. Prepare the filling. Dice the pumpkin in 5 mm cubes and chop the onions finely, then keep them apart in two bowls.
4. Mix the filling. Just before shaping, stir the pumpkin and onions together with the oil, cumin, pepper and salt.
5. Roll the dough. Cut it in 4 pieces and roll each one on a floured surface to 2 mm thin, then cut it into squares of about 10 cm, 24 in all.
6. Fill the manty. Put a heaped tablespoon of filling in the middle of each square, lift the four corners together over the filling and pinch the seams shut.
7. Prepare the steamer. Oil each tier of the steamer well, put a pot of water underneath and bring it to the boil.
8. Steam the manty. Lay the manty in the tiers with a finger's width between them, cover and steam over a steady boil for 30 minutes, until the dough is glossy and the pumpkin is soft.
9. Make the chile oil. While they steam, warm the 3 tablespoons of oil for 2 minutes in a small pan, then pour it over the chile flakes in a saucer.
10. Serve hot. Lift the manty onto plates with a spatula, sprinkle with walnuts and dill and bring them to the table with the chile oil.
