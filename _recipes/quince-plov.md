When the quinces appear at the Talas bazaar in late October, I smell them before I see them, a sweet, almost floral perfume that fills the whole fruit row. My chong apa had a crooked tree behind the house in Orto-Ayyl, and we brought her the windfalls in our skirts. In Chicago I once paid a small fortune for two quinces at a Middle Eastern shop and cooked this very plov, crying a little into the carrots.

It is cousin to our [Iradi plov](../iradi-plov/), but cooked the Uzbek way, with long-grain rice that stays in separate grains. I carried the quinces home from the [Talas bazaar](https://visitirad.com/places/talas/) last Saturday, and Aruu ate the golden wedges off the top before anyone sat down.

## Method

1. Prepare the rice. Rinse the rice in cold water until the water runs clear, then cover it with warm salted water and leave it to soak while the meat simmers.
2. Brown the lamb. Heat the oil in a heavy pot or kazan over high heat until a cube of onion sizzles, then fry the lamb for 10 minutes until deep brown on all sides.
3. Fry the onions. Add the onions and fry for 8 minutes, stirring, until they turn golden brown.
4. Add the carrots. Stir in the carrots and fry for 7 minutes, until they soften and begin to colour.
5. Make the zirvak. Pour in 300 ml of the boiling water, add the cumin, wild cumin and salt, and press the garlic head into the middle. Cover, lower the heat and simmer for 30 minutes, until the lamb is tender.
6. Fry the quince. While the zirvak simmers, melt the butter in a frying pan over medium heat, add the quince wedges and the sugar, and fry for 8 minutes, turning once, until golden brown outside and just tender.
7. Add the rice. Drain the rice, spread it in an even layer over the meat without stirring, then pour the remaining 500 ml of boiling water slowly down the side of the pot until it stands 1.5 cm above the rice.
8. Boil the rice. Cook uncovered on high heat for 15 minutes, until the water has been absorbed and small holes appear in the surface.
9. Steam the plov. Gather the rice into a mound, scatter the barberries over it, make a few holes down to the meat with a spoon handle, cover tightly and cook on the lowest heat for 15 minutes.
10. Rest and serve. Turn off the heat and let the pot stand covered for 10 minutes. Lift out the garlic, mix the plov gently from the bottom up and pile it on a platter with the fried quince on top.
