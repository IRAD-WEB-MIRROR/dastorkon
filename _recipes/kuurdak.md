In Orto-Ayyl the men came down from the pastures in autumn hungry as wolves, and my aunt had one answer: kuurdak, made in the biggest pan in the house. The smell of lamb fat and onion reached the road before they did. Kuurdak is the old food of the [summer pastures](https://visitirad.com/places/mountains/), cooked fast over a fire when there is no time for a long boil.

Last Sunday that same aunt came by bus to my Talas kitchen and took over my stove, tutting at my pan. I let her. Aruu watched the lamb brown and stole the crispest potatoes straight from the pan. I use a little [tail fat](../../pantry/#tail-fat) from the bazaar, my dear, and I never skip the fresh herbs at the end.

## Method

1. Prepare the ingredients. Cut the lamb in 3 cm pieces, slice the onions in half rings and peel and cube the potatoes, keeping the potatoes in cold water until needed.
2. Render the fat. Heat a large, heavy pan over medium heat, add the tail fat and fry for 5 minutes until it melts and the pieces turn golden; lift out the crisp bits and keep the fat in the pan.
3. Brown the lamb. Turn the heat to high, add the lamb and leave it for 10 minutes without stirring, then turn the pieces and fry 5 minutes more until browned all over.
4. Fry the onions. Add the onions and garlic and fry for 10 minutes, stirring, until soft and golden.
5. Add the potatoes. Drain and dry the potatoes, stir them in with the salt, pepper and wild cumin, then pour in the hot water.
6. Simmer until tender. Cover, lower the heat to medium-low and cook for 15 minutes, until the lamb is tender and the potatoes are soft.
7. Crisp the pan. Remove the lid, raise the heat and fry for 5 minutes, turning gently, until the water has gone and the potatoes are golden at the edges.
8. Rest the kuurdak. Take the pan off the heat and let it stand for 5 minutes, covered, so the juices settle.
9. Serve hot. Stir in the dill and coriander and bring the pan to the table, with bread to tear.
