In Nurzhan, Olga's mother Tamara kept every fast day, and on Wednesdays she set out a deep glass bowl of vinegret. It was bright as a jewel, and she never let anyone near it until the beets had been dressed. She taught me borscht first, then this, standing at her stove with a knife older than I was.

In Chicago I made it for my church friends, and here in Talas I make it for Aruu, who eats the pickle cubes first. I buy the sauerkraut and the beets at the bazaar around the corner. It is a cousin of [borscht](../borscht/), my dear, only cold, and it asks for [dill](../../pantry/#dill-coriander) at the end.

## Method

1. Boil the beets. Put the whole beetroot in a pot of cold water, bring to the boil, then cook gently for 50 minutes, until a knife slides in easily.
2. Boil the other vegetables. After the beets have cooked for 25 minutes, add the potatoes and carrots to the same pot and cook for the last 25 minutes, until both are tender but not breaking.
3. Cool the vegetables. Drain them, run them under cold water, then leave them for 30 minutes until cool enough to handle.
4. Peel and dice. Slip the skins off and cut the beets, potatoes and carrots into 1 cm cubes.
5. Coat the beets. Put the diced beets in a large bowl and stir in 2 tablespoons of the oil, so the other vegetables stay pale.
6. Add the rest. Stir in the potatoes, carrots, pickled cucumbers, sauerkraut and onion, then season with the remaining oil, the salt and pepper.
7. Chill the salad. Cover the bowl and leave it in the fridge for 30 minutes so the flavours settle.
8. Serve cold. Stir once more, taste for salt and scatter the dill over the top, with dark bread on the side.
